How every study is written

Three questions, answered in the same order every time.

So you can compare them, and so you can tell when a number is doing more work than it should.

  • What was the situation?

    What the client was doing before, what they were spending, and what they could and could not see. Usually the answer is a boosted post and a pixel firing on the wrong page.

  • What was done first?

    Always the measurement. Every study names which events were rebuilt and what they were reconciled against before any budget moved.

  • What was counted, and how?

    The result, the source it came from, and what it is not. If leases were not tracked as conversions, the page says so and shows the influenced-value math instead.

  • What was not tracked?

    Named on every page. A number you can divide is worth more than a number you have to trust, and the honest gaps are what make the rest believable.

Reading the headline number

What "conservative floor" means, and why the page says it.

The property management ads promoted listings that later leased, but signed leases were not tracked as direct Meta conversions. So the return shown is influenced value compared with spend, counted as one month's commission plus the application fee, with most rents individually confirmed. Not proven last-click return on ad spend. It is the lowest number the evidence supports, which is why it is the one on the page.
