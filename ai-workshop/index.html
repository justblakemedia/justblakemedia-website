Build 1

Email Drafter - Skill

Make a guide from emails you wrote. Use it to draft new emails in your voice.

Choose your examples

Use one route:

  • Paste sent emails. Paste 3 emails of one type. Pick one type: donor thank-yous OR partner follow-ups. Do not mix types. Use only your words. Remove names, gift amounts, quoted replies, and signatures.
  • Use connected email. Only if your org allows it. The prompt will ask which account to use. Check the list before you approve it.

Build and save it

  1. Open Copy build prompt below. Click Copy. Paste it into your AI chat.
  2. Fill in the [brackets]. For example, [one type of email] becomes donor thank-yous. Add your 3 emails. Send.
  3. If you chose connected email, check the list it shows before you approve it.
  4. Read the draft guide. Tell it what to change. When you like it, say: Give me the final guide.
  5. Save the final guide in a document named Email Drafter.
  6. Test it. Open a new chat. Paste your saved guide. Open Copy test prompt below. Click Copy. Fill in a real task. Send. Fix the guide if the draft sounds wrong.

Next step, optional: After you build it, you can save your guide in a Project so you do not paste it each time. In your AI tool, go to Projects, New Project, paste your guide into Instructions, Save.

If you get stuck: Copy failed? Select the prompt text and paste it. No Projects? Save in a document and paste it into each new chat.

build 1

If I paste emails, use only my words. Ignore quoted replies, forwarded text, signatures, and text written by others. Then select up to 15 of my sent messages from the last 45 days. Show me the selected subjects or a brief description. Wait for me to approve or replace the selection before using their contents or writing the guide. Use my words in replies, but ignore the quoted conversation and private details. If email access is unavailable, tell me and ask me to paste examples instead.

[Paste your cleaned sent emails here if you chose that route.]

Write a short guide with 5 to 8 rules for my openings, sentence length, tone, requests, closings, and phrases to avoid. Include three short, redacted excerpts that show the rules. Use only what my examples support. Ask me about any pattern you cannot tell from them.

Show me the guide for corrections. Wait for my reply. When I say “Give me the final guide,” return the complete final guide so I can save it. If I request changes, revise the guide before giving me the final version. Tell me how this app can reuse it, if it has that feature. Do not claim it is saved until I check. Do not send any email.

Build 3

Chief of Staff - Agent

Make a short brief of today's meetings, tasks, and email.

Choose your sources

Chief of Staff is for people who do not mind connecting their apps. Select the ones you use:

  • Email
  • Calendar
  • Note-taker
  • Task manager

Connect the ones you use in your AI tool. Use only accounts you are allowed to use.

Connect your apps. Do this before you copy the prompt.

  1. Open Settings in your AI tool.
  2. Find Apps, Connectors, or Extensions → Click Connect.
  3. Pick Email first, then Calendar, then Note-taker or Task manager if you use them.

You are connected when you see your email listed. Use another tool? Ask for help.

Time zone example: America/New_York.

Build and save it

  1. Open Copy build prompt below. Click Copy. Paste it into your AI chat.
  2. Fill in your time zone, like America/New_York. Select your sources: email, calendar, note-taker, task manager. Send.
  3. Tell it to research your sources for today. Check the brief against your real calendar and tasks. Fix anything wrong.
  4. Read the draft. Tell it what to change. When you like it, say: Give me the final briefing instructions.
  5. Save the final instructions, not today's brief, in a document named Chief of Staff.
  6. Test it. Open a new chat. Paste your saved instructions. Open Copy test prompt below. Click Copy. Fill in your time zone. Send. Check the new brief.
  7. Scheduling is for later. Do not set it up today.

Next step, optional: After you build it, you can save your instructions in a Project so you do not paste them each time. In your AI tool, go to Projects, New Project, paste your instructions into Instructions, Save.

If you get stuck: Cannot connect? Pick Email Drafter or Social Poster instead. Time zone rejected? Type America/New_York.

Proposed local time: [add or say not applicable]
Proposed private destination: [add or say not applicable]

First check which sources this app can actually read. Tell me what I still need to connect. If my time zone is missing or still bracketed, ask me for it before drafting. Wait until I connect the chosen sources before making today's brief. Do not claim access to an account until you can read a current item from it. Do not claim a schedule exists until I can open it.

Make today's brief from sources you actually checked. Include the date and time checked, up to three priorities with next steps and source links where available, today's meetings in my time zone, upcoming deadlines, work waiting on others, and sources you could not check. Keep emails, tasks, and meetings separate: do not assume items are related because names or timing match. Connect them only when the sources provide evidence. Label any proposed next step as a suggestion, separate from a recorded task or commitment. Check later replies and completed tasks before calling something open. Show me today's brief and ask for corrections. Wait for my reply. When I say “Give me the final briefing instructions,” return the complete reusable instructions separately from today's dated brief so I can save them. If I request changes, revise the brief and instructions before giving me the final version. Do not create a schedule today. If I later ask to schedule and this app supports scheduling and private delivery, show me the exact proposed setup and wait for my confirmation before creating one schedule. Only send the brief to the private destination I confirm. After setup, tell me where to open it and what to verify: sources, enabled status, days, time, time zone, destination, and next run. Otherwise, tell me how to run the saved instructions by opening a new chat. Do not describe a brief as scheduled or delivered until I can open the schedule.

Guardrails

What not to hand over.

AI output is a draft. Check facts, privacy, and wording before you use it.

  • Confidential and personal data

    Donor records, gift amounts, employee info, anything that identifies a private person.

    Check your org policy first. Use redacted examples today.

  • Facts, numbers, quotes, sources

    A model will invent a plausible number rather than leave a blank.

    Check every number and quote against your own source.

  • Confidence you have not earned

    It sounds certain even when it is wrong. Fluency is not accuracy.

    Ask what it is unsure about. Tell it to mark those spots [CHECK].

  • Your organization's voice

    Left alone, everything drifts to the same warm nonprofit tone donors skim.

    Use your voice guide. Keep a banned-words list in it.

  • Ownership and copyright

    Check your org rules for ownership, permissions, and attribution before you publish AI text or images.

    Keep your source and a record of your review.

  • The final approval

    The last read before a donor sees it is a judgment call about a relationship. That part is yours.

    Review every draft before it goes out.

Justin's stack

    Granola

    Joins every call and writes the notes. Transcripts go where my AI tools can read them.

    Wispr Flow
