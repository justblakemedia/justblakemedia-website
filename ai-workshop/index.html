  2. 02

    Add your website page

    Choose Upload a source when prompted, or Add sources on the left, then select Websites. Paste the full website address, including https:// or http://. Use a public page on your organization's site; a homepage works. Gemini Notebook imports that page's text, not the whole website.

    Watch and check it

    When it's ready, select the video title in Studio and press play. Compare its facts, names, and visuals with your website before you use or share it.

Build 1

Write emails that sound like you

Make a short guide from emails you actually wrote. Use it to draft donor, partner, or colleague emails in your voice.

Open the build prompt, fill in your route and readers, then paste it with your examples.

Choose your examples

Use one route:

  • Paste sent emails. Pick 3 to 5 emails you wrote. Choose a mix of the messages you want help with, such as donor stewardship or partner follow-ups. Copy only your words. Remove names, gift details, quoted replies, and signatures.
  • Use connected email. If your organization permits it and your AI app can read email, connect the work account you choose. The prompt asks for up to 15 of your sent messages from the last 45 days. Check what the app can access before it reads anything.

Build and save it

  1. Open an AI chat your organization allows. Open “Build this with AI” and click Copy. Replace the bracketed fields with your email route, readers, and email types. If you chose pasted emails, add the cleaned examples to the same message. Submit that message in the AI chat.
  2. If you chose connected email, choose the account and follow the app's connection steps only if that feature is available. Review the messages it selects. Replace any that are private or unlike the email types you want to draft.
  3. Read the “My Email Voice” guide. Correct anything that misses your openings, sentence length, requests, or closings.
  4. Copy the final guide into a document named “My Email Voice,” or save it in your app's reusable instructions if that feature exists. Open the saved version yourself to make sure it is there.
  5. Start a new chat. Make the guide available by opening the saved project or attaching or pasting the document. Open “Test it in a new chat,” fill in a real email task, then copy and paste that prompt. Revise the guide if the draft still sounds wrong.

Build 2

Capture your organization's social voice

Make a writing guide from your organization's own posts. Use it to draft a new post for a real program, campaign, or event.

Add your selected posts to the prompt before submitting it.

Choose your posts

Open a social account your organization uses. Pick 3 to 5 posts that sound like it, ideally across the kinds of work you write about. Copy the full captions or take readable screenshots. For each post, note the platform and intended audience. Remove private details before sharing.

Build and save it

  1. Open a new AI chat. Open “Build this with AI” and click Copy. Paste the prompt, then add your captions or upload screenshots where it asks for posts. Label each post with its platform and audience before submitting the message.
  2. Check that the AI read every caption, including text in screenshots. Supply missing or cut-off text before it writes the guide.
  3. Read its “Our Social Voice” guide. Correct the rules for tone, openings, length, formatting, and calls to action. Tell it when a pattern is specific to one platform.
  4. Copy the final guide into a document named “Our Social Voice,” or save it in the app's reusable instructions if available. Open the saved version to verify it.
  5. In a new chat, make the guide available. Open “Test it in a new chat,” fill in a real post task with current approved facts, and paste it. If the draft misses your voice, update the guide and test again.

Build 3

Get a daily brief

Make a short briefing from the work you need to see today: meetings, open tasks, and relevant email you can verify.

Open the build prompt, choose manual or scheduled use, and fill in your sources.

Choose your sources

Choose the work email and calendar you may use, plus a task list if you have one. Check your organization's rules before connecting accounts. If you cannot connect them, have a short, nonprivate list of today's meetings, deadlines, tasks, and messages ready to paste. Decide your days, local time, time zone, and private destination if you want a scheduled brief.

Build and save it

  1. Open an AI chat. Open “Build this with AI,” click Copy, replace the bracketed source fields, and choose “Manual only” or “Schedule after approval.” For a schedule, also fill in the days, time zone, and private destination. Paste the whole prompt.
  2. Check whether your app can read each chosen source. Connect only the accounts you choose. If it cannot read one, paste the relevant, nonprivate items in the same chat and label that source as manual.
  3. Once those sources are ready, request today's brief. Compare its meetings, tasks, deadlines, and source links with the actual records. Correct anything completed, missing, or assigned to the wrong person.
  4. Save the revised briefing instructions in a document named “Daily Brief,” or in a reusable app feature if available. Open the saved version to verify it.
  5. If you chose scheduling and the app supports it, review the proposed days, time zone, destination, and sources. Confirm that exact setup before the app creates it. Then open the saved schedule and check its enabled status and next run. Otherwise, use your saved instructions to request the brief manually.
  6. Use “Test it in a new chat” to check the saved instructions and setup. For a schedule, request one delivery test and check that it arrived at the destination. After the next scheduled time, check that a fresh brief arrived too.

Tool map

The major AI assistants, and where each fits.

Five assistants, plus one way to choose. Start with a tool your organization allows, then judge it on a real task. Saved instructions can begin as a document you paste into a new chat.

  • ChatGPT

    by OpenAI

    Good fit
    A familiar entry point for drafting, editing, brainstorming and everyday questions. The workshop's email demonstration uses ChatGPT; the exercise also works in an approved tool you already have.
    Free plan
    Yes. Free accounts can create Projects. Check your workspace settings before adding work material.
    Where to keep your instructions
    Save them in a Project, or paste them into each new chat.

    chatgpt.com

  • Claude

    by Anthropic

    Good fit
    Writing, careful edits and work that needs sustained attention. Anthropic also offers Claude Code for coding and Claude Cowork for working through tasks.
    Free plan
    Yes, and free accounts can create a small number of Projects.
    Where to keep your instructions
    Add them to a Project's instructions, or paste them into each new chat.

    claude.ai

  • Gemini

    by Google

    Good fit
    A practical fit when your work lives in Google. Gemini connects with Google products such as Drive and Docs and can help create images; access varies by product and plan.
    Free plan
    Yes, and free accounts can create Gems.
    Where to keep your instructions
    Try a Gem if your account offers it. Check its available file access before relying on Drive content.

    gemini.google.com

  • Copilot

    by Microsoft

    Good fit
    A practical fit for organizations using Microsoft 365, including Outlook, Word, Excel and Teams. Check which Copilot features your organization has enabled.
    Free plan
    The personal Copilot app is free. Copilot inside Microsoft 365 is a paid add-on your organization has to buy.
    Where to keep your instructions
    Paste them into each new chat. If your organization has Microsoft 365 Copilot, ask IT about reusable agents.

    copilot.microsoft.com

  • Grok

    by xAI

    Good fit
    Research and current conversation on X. Grok can work with live X information; check its sources and dates before using a claim.
    Free plan
    Yes, with limits. Not to be confused with Groq, a chip company
    Where to keep your instructions
    Paste them into each new chat.

    grok.com

  • How to choose

    Start here
    Pick one tool your organization allows and learn it on work you know.
    Compare with care
    Use approved, redacted work, give it the details it needs, and check the result for facts, voice and privacy.
    Keep what works
    Save the instructions you have reviewed. Product names and features change; the task and your judgment stay central.

Plan details last checked October 2026. Features and access vary by account and organization. Check what your app can do before relying on a saved guide, connection, or schedule.

Guardrails

What you should not hand over.

Your team handles donor and community relationships. Treat AI output as a draft, and check facts, privacy, and final wording before using it.

  • Confidential and personal data

    Donor records, gift amounts, employee information, anything that identifies a private individual. How your data is used depends on the app, account, and settings.

    Check your organization's policy before uploading records or connecting accounts. Use redacted examples for this workshop.

  • Facts, numbers, quotes, sources

    A model will fill a gap with something plausible rather than leave it blank. A fabricated statistic in an appeal is a correction you have to send to your whole list.

    Every number and every quote gets checked against your own source before it goes out.

  • Confidence you have not earned

    The tone is always certain, whether the answer is right or invented. Fluency is not accuracy, and it reads the same either way.

    Ask it what it is unsure about, and tell it to mark those spots with [CHECK].

  • Your organization's voice

    Left alone, everything drifts toward the same warm, padded nonprofit register. That register is exactly what your donors have learned to skim.

    The voice guide exists for this. Keep a banned-words list in it and update it when something slips through.

  • Ownership and copyright

    Before using AI-generated text or images publicly, check your organization's rules for ownership, permissions, and attribution.

    Keep the source material and a record of your review before publishing.

  • The final approval

    The last read before something reaches a donor is a judgment call about a relationship. That is the part of the job that is actually yours.

    Review every draft and confirm any scheduled setup before it runs.

Justin's stack

What runs a one-person business, and why each piece earns its place.

Not a recommendation list. This is the actual setup. Copy the shape, not the tools.

  • Meetings

    Granola

    Sits in every call and writes the notes. Afterward the transcript syncs into the same place the AI tools read from.

    Every client conversation becomes a source of truth the AI can work from. No retyping, no "what did we agree to."

  • Voice to text

    Wispr Flow

    Hold a key, talk, and clean text lands wherever the cursor is. Emails, briefs, prompts.

    I'm a talker. Dictating an idea gets it out of my head faster than typing it, and I can edit the text once it's there.

  • The build layer

    Claude Code and Codex

    I use them to build applications, create and update websites, and make custom automations for repeatable work.

    They let me turn an idea into a working tool, then keep improving it as the work changes.

  • Connectors

    Gmail, Calendar, Drive, Slack

    The AI reads the calendar, the inbox, and the files it is allowed to see, and nothing else. Permissions are given per tool, on purpose.

    One sentence in, a finished Google Form out. It is closer than it looks.
