Where are you on the ladder?

Every task can climb one rung. You are not learning a tool, you are writing a job description for a new hire who never sleeps, then testing whether it does the work the way you would. Most of the value is on rung one, and most people skip it.

  1. Task

    You do it by hand, once, and notice every step and every judgment call you make.

  2. Job description

    The steps written down: who the AI is, who it writes to, how you sound, what good looks like. Paste it in. Now it repeats.

  3. Agent

    The job description gets real inputs on its own: your inbox, your calendar, a form. It runs, you review.

  4. Automation

    On a schedule or a trigger. It runs without you in the loop, and you check the output.

  • It sounds like everyone else.

    The fix

    A voice document the system reads before it writes a word. Your bans, your openings, your numbers rule. The output stops sounding like a press release because the rules say it cannot.

  • It does not know my business.

    The fix

    Your context lives somewhere the AI can reach: clients, rates, what happened on the last call. Not retyped into every prompt, kept once and read every time.

  • I cannot trust it to run on its own.

    The fix

    Approval gates. Anything that spends, sends or publishes is created paused and waits for a person. The system proposes, with the dollar impact. You confirm.

The questions I get asked most, answered.

The same questions come up on every first call. Here they are, so the call can start further along.

What is the difference between boosting a post and running ads?

Boosting pays to show one post to more people, with almost no control over who sees it or what happens after they do. Running ads in Ads Manager lets you choose the objective, build the audience, test several creatives against each other, and track what people did on your site.

A campaign adds structure on top: budgets that follow results, and reporting you can defend. The table above shows the three side by side.

Which advertising platforms do you work in?

Meta, which covers Facebook and Instagram, is the core. LinkedIn for B2B demand generation. I have also run Snapchat and Pinterest. I do not sell Google search as a standalone service, and if search is all you run, I will tell you on the first call.

What does it cost to start?

Every engagement starts with one paid, fixed-fee step. The Foundations Sprint is $3,500 over 30 days for paid media. The AI Clarity Session is $1,500 over one week for automation. Ongoing management is monthly, banded to your ad spend, and quoted on the call once there is something worth managing.

What actually happens in month one?

Tracking gets audited and rebuilt before any budget moves, because everything after that depends on measuring it. Then the account structure, then creative. By day 30 you have a rebuilt account, a 90-day roadmap, and a baseline you can put in front of a board.

Why not an agency?

An agency sells you a senior name at the pitch and staffs a junior on the account. I am the person in the pitch and the person in the account, and there is nobody between us.

Where do I start with AI if I have tried it and it was underwhelming?

Pick the one thing that ate your week, and hand the boring half of it to AI without losing your voice. Do the task by hand once, write the steps down as a job description, and test whether the tool does it the way you would. That is rung one on the ladder above, and it is where the value is. Copying someone else's stack is how most teams end up with a subscription and no change.

Who are you wrong for?

If you only run Google search, if you want one person doing content and paid media together, or if you need someone in your office every week, I will say so on the first call instead of the third. Under $5,000 a month in ad spend, Amplify is the better fit.

Do you run workshops?

Yes. Two subjects, paid social fundamentals and AI for marketing teams, run as a single session or a short series for a company, or as one-to-one coaching. Every workshop ends with the same thing: a plan you can act on Monday, not a tool list.
