The Problem

When you run ten programs, you can't run ten campaigns the same way.

When this engagement started, three programs had active promotion and seven were dark. JLearn had not run paid ads in over eight months. Inclusive Futures had never had a paid campaign. Two programs were competing for the same Facebook audience. Gap Year Fellowship and JTeen were both targeting young Atlanta adults with no audience separation between them.

The campaign structure that works for awareness of a teen leadership program is the wrong structure for driving applications to a gap year fellowship. The audience that responds to a security education message is not the same audience that signs up for early childhood literacy. Running every program through a single generalist campaign means everyone gets the wrong message. Budget gets wasted. Programs underperform. The community doesn't know half of what the federation actually offers.

And then there's the donor risk. This federation has built relationships with monthly givers, major donors, and community members who have been supporting these programs for years. Showing a fundraising ad to someone who already donated last week doesn't just waste budget. It erodes trust. The community knows when they're being over-targeted. That erosion is real and it's slow, which makes it more dangerous.

The Risk of Getting This Wrong

  • Running all programs to the same audience creates message fatigue across the entire community
  • Reaching existing donors with acquisition campaigns damages relationships built over years
  • Under-promoting programs because coordination is too complex leaves a real community impact gap
  • Over-spending on the wrong audiences burns through a nonprofit budget that has nowhere to waste
The Strategy

Two tracks. One system. Every program in its right place.

The solution wasn't more ads. It was a structure that let six programs run simultaneously without competing with each other, or with the people who already fund them.

Awareness Campaign

Federation Awareness

Purpose: Build program awareness with community members who haven't engaged yet
Audience: Cold audiences + lookalike audiences (1% seed)
Donor database: 🚫 Fully suppressed — existing donors excluded
Format: Carousel ads with LEARN MORE CTA
Programs
JTeen — Hadassah Chesed Awards (5-card carousel)
PJ Library — International Conference (3-card carousel)
Gap Year Fellowship — Tomorrow's Leaders (5-card carousel)

Why Suppression Matters

The awareness campaign runs to people who don't know this federation well yet. Existing donors — the people who already give — are excluded entirely. They don't need an awareness campaign. They need stewardship. Separating these audiences isn't a technical detail. It's how you protect years of relationship-building from a three-week ad flight.

The Results

What happens when every program gets its own lane.

6 Programs running simultaneously — first time in federation history
11,328 People reached, Inclusive Futures Parent Cohort 2 (June 2026, its strongest program)
0 Existing donors shown a cold acquisition ad

June 2026 reach by program, Meta-reported: Parent Cohort 2 (Inclusive Futures) 11,328; PJ Library 7,747; Cyber Safety (Community Security) 7,523; Gap Year Fellowship 5,437; JTeen 2,223; JLearn 766. The flight launched May 19, so full-flight totals run higher than these June figures. None of the six ad sets track applications, downloads, or inquiries as a distinct event today, so this case study reports reach and structure rather than a conversion count it can't yet back with data. The structural outcome is what holds regardless: six programs in front of the right people at the same time, with the donor base fully protected by suppression built into the awareness ad set itself.

The Impact

From one campaign to a full program architecture.

For the first time, every program was visible at the same time. Parents of children with developmental disabilities saw Inclusive Futures. High schoolers saw JTeen. Monthly donors never saw a cold ask. Young adults with no prior connection to the federation saw Gap Year Fellowship — and none of those impressions touched anyone who already gives.

Six programs running simultaneously is the structural outcome. The audience separation is what makes it sustainable. A family discovering PJ Library for the first time won't see a Gap Year Fellowship ad. A monthly donor won't see a cold acquisition ask. The federation's complexity became an advantage instead of a liability — and the system is repeatable for every flight that follows.
